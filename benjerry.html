01 — The Brief

Same soul.
Sharper system.

Ben & Jerry's current packaging is beloved — but it has a problem. On a freezer shelf, everything competes. Their pints have become so visually busy that they're hard to scan. The illustration, the logo, the flavour name, the ingredient callouts, the brand mission, the Fair Trade badge — all competing on a 4-inch surface.

The redesign challenge: keep everything that makes Ben & Jerry's feel like Ben & Jerry's — the joy, the cause, the Vermont roots — but give each pint a single dominant visual idea. One pint, one story, told with type and colour.

"The flavour name IS the design.
The colour IS the identity.
The mission IS on the back."

03 — The System

Three pints.
One set of rules.

What makes this a system instead of just three individual designs is the rules. Every future Ben & Jerry's pint in Scoop Stories follows these constraints. The constraints are what give the system coherence.

🔤
Typography
Bebas Neue for the flavour name — nothing else. The word repeats twice: once at full opacity, once at 8% as a shadow layer behind it. Creates depth without illustration.
🎨
Colour
Lid colour = the flavour's dominant colour. Body uses a dark gradient of the same hue. Type uses a highlight tone of the same colour family. Never use colours unrelated to the flavour.
🏷️
Information hierarchy
1. Flavour name — fills the pint. 2. Ben & Jerry's badge — top right corner, always. 3. Ingredients and credentials — bottom band, always. Mission — back panel, proudly.
04 — Mission on the Surface

Values you can
see from the shelf.

Ben & Jerry's mission isn't a footnote. In the Scoop Stories system, the back panel of every pint is a full mission statement — big type, same design language, no apologies. The cause gets as much real estate as the flavour.

🌍
Climate Action
Back panel: "Made with Fair Trade ingredients because the planet is everyone's favourite flavour." Large Bebas Neue type. QR code to campaign page.
🐄
Vermont Farms
Bottom band of every pint: "Milk from Vermont family farms." Consistent on all three flavours — not hidden in 6pt type on the side.
✊
Flavour × Cause
Cherry Garcia → supports music education. Half Baked → criminal justice reform. Maple Moose → Indigenous land rights in Canada.
♻️
Sustainable Packaging
100% recyclable FSC-certified paperboard. Clear recycling instructions on the base. No plastic wrap on the lid. Less ink coverage — more sustainable to print.
05 — What I Learned

Packaging is a
360° problem.

1
The shelf is the context.
A pint that looks great in isolation can disappear in a freezer aisle. The redesign prioritises contrast, size, and colour saturation specifically for the 10-foot view — what you see through glass, from across the store. That's the real brief.
2
The lid is the first impression.
In a stacked freezer, the lid is all you see. The current Ben & Jerry's lid is illustrated and complex — stunning up close, illegible at a glance. A solid colour lid with the flavour name reads instantly from any angle. That single decision changes the entire shelf experience.
3
Brand equity is a constraint, not a starting point.
You can't redesign Ben & Jerry's without Ben & Jerry's. The playfulness, the cause, the Vermont roots — these aren't optional. Every constraint the brand imposes is a design decision that had to be respected, not overridden. The best redesigns feel inevitable, not imposed.
